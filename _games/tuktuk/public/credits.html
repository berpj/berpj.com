Real streets.
Original arcade spirit.

A local browser game built with Three.js, Rapier, TypeScript and original procedural art. Bangkok's street layout, building footprints and elevated railway alignment come from OpenStreetMap.

Open map data

© OpenStreetMap contributors. Map data is available under the Open Database License. The game’s road widths, building heights and architectural detailing include authored estimates. Gameplay anchors and supplemental shophouses are fictional. The playable district is a compact crop; mapped parks are kept open.

Materials and artwork

Surface textures from Poly Haven, licensed CC0. Asset credits, source links and hashes · Additional surface maps. Tuk-tuk, traffic, characters, shop signs and street furniture are original code-generated meshes. The shophouse facade texture is original AI-generated artwork; its generation prompt and provenance are included. Twelve shop styles (massage, barber, tailor, pizza, laundry, vacant shutters, noodles, coffee, bakery, florist, hardware and fruit) were made with built-in image generation; their complete prompts and provenance are included. Business names and placements are fictional. Environmental art is a stylized interpretation of Bangkok, rather than a photographically exact reconstruction.

Daylight sky

Kloofendal 48d Partly Cloudy (Pure Sky) by Greg Zaal and Jarod Guest, CC0. Used for the sky and reflections, with sunlight aligned to the photograph. This is atmospheric artwork, not a sky photograph taken in Bangkok. Source files and hashes.

Open-source libraries

Three.js, MIT · Rapier, Apache 2.0 · Vite, MIT · TypeScript, Apache 2.0

polygon-clipping, MIT, used to keep building footprints clear of drivable roads. Clipper, Boost Software License, is used offline to bake non-overlapping road surfaces.

Thai radio

Piphat in Wat Khung Taphao, recorded by Tevaprapas (เทวประภาส มากคล้าย), 2007. A real traditional Thai ensemble recorded in Uttaradit, used as the tuk-tuk radio; this is not a Bangkok location recording or AI-generated music. Licensed Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported. The full performance was converted to mono MP3, with rumble reduction, loudness normalization and short fades. This audio adaptation is shared under the same CC BY-SA 3.0 license. No endorsement is implied.

Sound effects and typography

The horn uses a trimmed recording of NSU Prinz - troblja by Work With Sounds / Technical Museum of Slovenia, recorded by Boštjan Troha (2015), under CC BY 4.0. Converted to mono PCM, filtered, faded and level-adjusted. This is a vintage vehicle-horn sample, not a Bangkok tuk-tuk field recording. Horn source and processing · Listen to the horn. Engine and other driving effects use original Web Audio synthesis. Barlow typeface by Jeremy Tribby, licensed under the SIL Open Font License. The game is inspired by arcade taxi-driving mechanics and uses original branding and assets.
